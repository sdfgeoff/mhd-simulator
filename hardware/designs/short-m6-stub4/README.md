# Short M6 nozzle: raised melt pool

1. This replaces the long low-pool waist as the preferred **geometry concept**.
   The thread envelope remains M6×1 with 6 mm engagement, but the overall nozzle
   is 10 mm long, with only 4 mm below the heater. The flat-sided lower body is
   6 mm across X and 2.8 mm across Y, with a 2 mm lower bore. Its nominal 0.4 mm
   outlet and 0.7 mm square tip retain a 0.15 mm wall. Thread roots/helix, tool
   radii, tolerances, contact clamps and magnet mounts are not detailed.
2. The open pool surface is 2 mm inside the heater. A separate steady-conduction
   screen finds 2.47 K/W for this short body with its raised pool,
   versus 37 K/W for the old long low pool. It assumes ideal heater contact and
   an isothermal pool; it does not predict local liquid temperature, wire melting,
   repeated operation or heater power. Feeding and heat losses remain unresolved.
3. Four stock 25×5×5 mm SmCo bars form two poles with a 3 mm gap. The bars extend
   nominally 1.45 mm below the outlet, excluding mounts and tolerances. The fixed
   source assumption is Br=0.95 T and unit relative permeability. Hot magnet grade,
   heat isolation and demagnetization are not qualified.
4. Native 50 µm static terminal-current screening gives about 0.50 T mean outlet
   transverse field and 2.64 mN downward outlet-region force at 30 A. Increasing
   source subdivisions from 3 to 4 changes this force by 0.73%. No droplet outcome
   is established. Current does not imply a safe physical operating command.
5. Nozzle plus modeled copper resistance is 2.26 mΩ, using fixed hot-property
   stainless conductivity 1.1 MS/m and ideal joints. Real contact heating is absent.
   The electrical domain cuts the copper at x=±3.8 mm; CAD extends to ±5 mm.
6. Open `stub4.mhd.json` for CAD/material inspection. Direct electrode current still
   requires the separate research client; GUI Simulate does not reproduce it.
   Slow capillary motion remains under qualification. This is not a manufacturing
   drawing or a demonstrated hundreds-of-drops-per-second head.

```sh
target/debug/examples/conduction_probe \
  hardware/designs/short-m6-stub4/stub4.mhd.json 30 NEW_OUTPUT 0 x-ends 1e-6
```

The complete four-case sweep is generated by `scripts/make_stub_m6_cases.py`.
See `hardware/m6-stub-cold-screening.json`, `hardware/m6-stub-source-check.json`
and `hardware/heater-pool-conductance-screening.json` for evidence and limits.

A separate native 50 µm one-shot replay at 90 A peaks near 258 °C from 250 °C.
For its 250 µs positive lobe and documented reverse lobe, the assumed 200 Hz
RMS is 19.2 A, with about 0.96 W nozzle plus full-CAD copper estimate. These are
fixed-path/ideal-contact screens, not measured pulse settings or repetitive limits.
