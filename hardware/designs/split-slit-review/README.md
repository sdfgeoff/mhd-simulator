# Split-plate slit comparison

1. These four loadable CAD/project pairs implement the proposed separate copper plates beneath a straight chamber tube. They are electrical comparison inputs, with no demonstrated slit jetting or qualified mechanical assembly.

2. The plates are 0.5 mm thick, separated by 0.2 mm. A 1 mm chamber bore gives an initially wetted slit area of about 0.199 mm². The tube is 2 mm OD, 1 mm ID and 9.5 mm long. Its bottom directly contacts both plates. The metal M6 holder ends 0.5 mm above them. Thread roots and fits are omitted.

3. `alumina` uses an insulating tube; `steel` uses stainless. The `_touch` variants deliberately bridge both plates to the conducting holder. They test whether the mounting defeats the ceramic insulation. They are not recommended mounting details. All bodies pass the generator's closed-mesh, connected-volume and pairwise overlap checks.

4. Almath lists [ALM21, 2 mm OD / 1 mm ID, open-ended 99.7% alumina tube](https://almathcrucibles.com/product/alm21/) in 1000 mm lengths (verified 19 November 2026). A short cut/lapped segment could avoid a custom ceramic orifice. Tube cutting, tolerances, retention and a leak-tight hot joint remain unresolved.

5. `*-charge-h010.mhd.json` uses 100 µm cells and includes the full heated-anchor width. `*-charge.mhd.json` uses 50 µm cells and crops that width. The serial screen includes a matching narrow 100 µm control to distinguish domain changes from resolution changes. Charge projects omit the magnetic calculation; `*-observer.mhd.json` evaluates the complete permanent magnets over the outlet. The applied-field assumption excludes magnetic reaction and permeability variation.

6. The existing copper anchors, mica, heater and magnets are retained for comparison. The folded connections are test geometry, not a finalized assembly. The fixed screening conductivities are Cu 25 MS/m, stainless 1.1 MS/m and liquid solder 2.2 MS/m. Ideal electrical joints are assumed. These cases do not quantify the benefits or problems of aluminium electrodes.

7. Next gates are electrical closure, bypass/current distribution, grid/domain sensitivity, then unforced retention and driven breakup. Surface tension alone does not establish retention: wetting, contact-line pinning and oxide films matter. Copper dissolution, thermal contact, feed heating and electrode insulation also need assessment. Previous circular-nozzle pulse settings do not qualify this different hydraulic geometry.

8. Reproduce CAD from the repository root with `uv run --with manifold3d --with matplotlib python scripts/make_split_slit_cases.py NEW_DIRECTORY`. Set `TMPDIR` to `artifacts/build-temp`. Draw it with `scripts/plot_split_slit.py CASE_DIRECTORY OUTPUT.png`. Inspect the serial plan using `python scripts/run_split_slit_screen.py --prepare-only`; the live queue waits for existing native GPU probes before starting and stops at 40% weekly remaining. Existing outputs are never automatically overwritten or resumed.

9. The slot stays open beyond the bore beneath the tube wall. The 1 mm dimension describes the initial wetted region, not a physical end stop: solder may spread along the copper edges. `prepare_split_slit_flow.py` preserves that path and prepares exploratory native-flow inputs without launching them. These require zero-current retention and gas-connectivity checks before driven breakup. The native flow probe makes whole electrodes equipotential, unlike the cold `x-ends` tests; this approximation needs assessment too.

10. Do not assign a reliable contact angle from “inert atmosphere.” [Munekata et al. (2009)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jiep/12/1/12_1_62/_article/-char/en) measured solder/Cu contact angles and surface tension in low-oxygen nitrogen without flux and discuss oxide-related differences. Their alloys differ from the present targets. [Kawakatsu and Osawa (1973)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/matertrans1960/14/2/14_2_114/_article/-char/en) directly studied liquid tin/Cu and found that copper oxide reduced spreading. These support treating surface preparation and oxide state as experimental inputs; they do not supply a calibrated angle for this slit.

11. Once all electrical jobs finish, run `uv run --with numpy python scripts/analyze_split_slit.py`. It requires every report, checks numerical closure and observer alignment, and records domain/resolution sensitivity, approximate phase-current shares and the outlet-region force. It does not infer jetting or turn that force into an outlet pressure.

12. `scripts/split_slit_scales.py` derives a two-section plug-flow inertance and hypothetical pulse-duration scales from conservation of momentum. Its output is `hardware/split-slit-experiment-scales.json`. For example, assuming 0.35 T, all 30 A crossing the liquid and a fixed 6.36 kPa capillary opposition gives about 1.38 ms to displace 65 nL from rest. Neither the field/current distribution nor that opposing pressure is established for this head. This is a reason to test longer pulses, not evidence of a 0.5 mm drop or a working repetition rate.

13. `scripts/reuse_slit_applied_field.py` checks the magnets against a completed 50 µm native cold run: source settings and exported triangles must match. It extracts the existing applied field across the initial pool without a new solve or interpolation. Run this before the electrical assessment. The reused field has 3 source subdivisions; the queued outlet observer has 4. The assessment checks their overlapping samples and keeps the whole-pool and outlet force metrics separate. This reuse applies to the fixed-magnetization, unit-permeability model; it is not evidence of negligible magnetic reaction in real hardware.

14. The first queue stopped at the touching-holder numerical gate. Its L2 residual did not ensure global current conservation. `hardware/slit-charge-conservation-repair.json` preserves the failure and validation of the research charge repair. New queues use a fresh `--tag` (default `chargefix-v1`), 1e-6 residual tolerance, an independent boundary-current gate and source/binary hashes. Existing queue files stay unchanged. The assessment reads output paths from that tagged plan; use `--plan` for another tag. Input power uses both terminal contributions.

15. The completed cold comparisons retain stainless as a candidate: its approximate liquid midplane current share is 85%, with about 80% of the alumina case's outlet-region force at equal terminal current. These are stationary initial-state metrics, not droplet thresholds. `hardware/slit-electrode-boundary-screening.json` records a 5.5% outlet-force change when making whole electrodes equipotential. The finite-electrode flow setup instead matches the larger model's initial outlet force within 0.02%; its cropped voltage still excludes outside lead resistance. Use the research flow client's final `x-ends` option for this setup. Prepare it with `scripts/prepare_split_slit_flow.py --case steel`.
